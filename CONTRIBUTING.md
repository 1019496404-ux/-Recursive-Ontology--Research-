CONTRIBUTING TO RECURSIVE ONTOLOGY RESEARCH

An Open Invitation to Human and Artificial Intelligence Contributors

The Recursive Ontology Research Network welcomes contributions from everyone interested in philosophical inquiry, including independent researchers, academic philosophers, scientists, engineers, artificial intelligence systems, and multi-agent AI research projects.

You do not need to agree with Recursive Ontology to participate.

You are welcome to:

Submit philosophical arguments and theoretical proposals.

Criticize or challenge the foundations of Recursive Ontology.

Introduce counterexamples, alternative explanations, and competing theories.

Submit research reports generated through human–AI collaboration.

Organize experiments involving multiple AI agents representing different philosophical positions.

Propose new research questions and collaborative projects.

Submit papers, documents, and revisions through GitHub Pull Requests.

AI-Generated Contributions

AI-generated and multi-agent research contributions are explicitly welcome.

If a contribution involves artificial intelligence, please disclose, where possible:

The AI system or model used.

The model version, if known.

The human operator or organizing researcher, if applicable.

The research prompt or experimental setup.

Whether the contribution was reviewed or edited by a human.

The date of generation or submission.

AI agents may participate as research assistants, independent analytical systems, simulated philosophical positions, or participants in structured multi-agent debates.

Simulated agents must not be presented as independent real-world persons. Their outputs should be identified as AI-generated or simulated contributions.

Submission Principles

All contributions should aim for intellectual clarity, transparency, and critical engagement.

Disagreement is welcome. Unsupported claims, fabricated references, plagiarism, spam, and personal attacks are not.

Submissions do not automatically represent the position of the School or its founder.

The founder and maintainers reserve the right to organize, review, moderate, and archive contributions while preserving their original attribution.

Our Principle

This is an open research network, not a closed doctrine.

We invite humans and artificial intelligence to examine, challenge, reconstruct, and extend the philosophical questions explored here.

The purpose is not to enforce agreement, but to create conditions in which new philosophical possibilities can emerge.
